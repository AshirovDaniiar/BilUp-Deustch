# A2 Stage 7B — Curriculum design, Units 12–20

**Status:** curriculum DESIGN for review and approval. These are **specifications**, not lessons: no production dialogue, reading text, exercise, question or translation is written here, and nothing is added to the A2 data modules. Unit 11 (pilot, authored) and all A1 content are unchanged; A2 stays `planned` and invisible. Nothing in this document is CEFR-approved; every level judgement is the author's and awaits a qualified reviewer (the same process as Unit 11, see `docs/review/`).

Companion documents: `A2_STAGE7B_VOCABULARY_MAP.md` (all words, productive vs. receptive) · `A2_STAGE7B_GRAMMAR_PROGRESSION.md` (concepts, prerequisites, recycling, B1 register) · `docs/implementation/A2_STAGE7B_REPORT.md` (checks, unresolved decisions).

## 0. How to read a unit specification

Each unit follows the Unit 11 template (28+ words, 2–3 grammar concepts, 2 listening dialogues, 1–2 readings, 1 speaking scenario, 1 writing task, exactly 8 revision exercises, exactly 15 final-test questions) and states:

* **Objectives O1–O5** — can-do statements (communicative outcomes).
* **Target tasks** — the real-life situations the unit prepares for.
* **Vocabulary** — size and emphasis; the words themselves are in the vocabulary map.
* **Grammar G1–G3** — concept ids (permanent, `a2-<kebab>`); details in the grammar progression.
* **L1–L2, R1–R2, S1, W1** — scenario specifications: setting, speakers, register, text type, communicative focus, what the questions test. *No wording.*
* **Revision exercises E1–E8** — type (fill / choice / order), concept, what it practises. Distribution in every unit: 3 fill, 3 choice, 2 order; at least 2 per concept. Revision exercises are practice with feedback; they are authored independently of the final test.
* **Final test T1–T15** — skill, objective, format. Distribution in every unit: **10 grammar, 3 vocabulary, 2 reading** (as in Unit 11); three options, one correct; authored positions of the correct answer 5/5/5 with no run of three; at least two items **recycle** earlier structures (marked ↻). Distractors must be real learner errors, never another grammatically correct answer (lesson learned in Stage 7A).
* **RU / KY considerations** and **dependencies**.

General rules for every unit: du/Sie register is fixed per text and consistent (Unit 11 decision F6); receptive vocabulary is never required in a gap, a speaking keyword or a writing requirement (F4); no B1 construction without a documented decision; both explanation languages authored separately, the Kyrgyz marked for native review.

## Unit 12 — Familie, Freunde und Zusammenleben

`a2-familie-freunde` · unit id 12 · **Purpose:** talk about the people around you — their character, how you get along, and how you share everyday life and favours — using pronouns and reflexive verbs.

**Objectives**
- **O1** Describe a friend's or relative's character and say how you are related.
- **O2** Talk about how people get along: meeting, getting to know each other, arguing, making up.
- **O3** Ask for, offer and thank for help; say what belongs to or pleases whom.
- **O4** Share household tasks with housemates and make simple requests.
- **O5** Report a social event in the past (*Wir haben uns … getroffen*).

**Target tasks:** introduce a friend to a colleague · phone a friend about a relative's wedding · agree household chores in a shared flat · write a note to a housemate · tell how you met your partner or best friend.

**Vocabulary:** **40 items** (35 productive, 5 receptive) in four groups: relatives and relationships · character · feelings · living together and the household. All unit-12 adjectives are *predicative* (adjective endings come in unit 13).

**Grammar**
- **G1** `a2-personalpronomen-akk-dat` — personal pronouns in Akkusativ and Dativ after verbs and prepositions. Prerequisites: A1 `akkusativ`, `dativ`, `dativ-prep`.
- **G2** `a2-dativverben-zwei-objekte` — verbs with Dativ (helfen, gehören, gefallen) and with two objects (schenken, geben, zeigen); noun + pronoun order. Prerequisite: G1.
- **G3** `a2-reflexive-verben` — reflexive pronoun, position, Perfekt. Prerequisites: A1 `verb-position`, `a2-perfekt-erfahrungen`.

**Listening**
- **L1** Phone call between two friends (informal, du): one reports on a cousin's wedding — who came, who got on well with whom. Focus: people and relationships, Perfekt with pronouns. Questions: who/what/why; one detail on a feeling.
- **L2** Two flatmates (informal, du) agree who does which chore this week and ask each other for favours. Focus: requests, offers, Dativ pronouns, a short argument resolved. Questions: assignment of tasks, reason, final agreement.

**Reading**
- **R1** Forum post "my best friend" (about 80 words): character, how they met, how they support each other. Tests: detail and attitude.
- **R2** Shared-flat chore plan (non-continuous: weekly table plus two short notes, about 60 words). Tests: scanning for who does what and when.

**Speaking**
- **S1** A new colleague (du) asks about your friends: describe one friend, how you met, what you do together; ask the partner the same. Four partner questions; hints use reflexive Perfekt and pronouns.

**Writing**
- **W1** Informal message to a housemate (about 50+ words): thank for help, ask a favour, divide two chores, one reason with `weil`, one reflexive verb, at least two pronouns.

**Revision exercises**

**RU / KY considerations**
- Kyrgyz kinship vocabulary is finer than German (older/younger sibling, maternal/paternal relatives); *Cousin, Großeltern, Verwandte, Bekannter* need an explicit gloss chosen by a native reviewer — no gloss may be invented here.
- Russian reflexives (-ся) parallel the German reflexive pronoun and can be used as a bridge; Kyrgyz expresses reflexivity/reciprocity with verb suffixes, so the explanation must describe the German word, not claim equivalence. Terminology (Russian «возвратные глаголы», Kyrgyz term) needs a native decision.
- Russian has Dativ and Accusative too (helps G1); Kyrgyz has a different case system, so case explanations use German examples first.

**Dependencies:** A1 units 2, 3, 4, 7; unit 11 (Perfekt, `weil`, reflexive chunks). Used later by 14 (reflexive imperative), 15 (*mir*), 16, 19, 20.

## Unit 13 — Wohnen und Nachbarschaft

`a2-wohnen-nachbarschaft` · unit id 13 · **Purpose:** find, describe and compare a flat, set it up, and live with neighbours — the first unit with adjective endings and comparison.

**Objectives**
- **O1** Understand and answer a housing advertisement; ask about rent, extra costs and deposit.
- **O2** Describe a flat or room and its furniture with correct adjective endings.
- **O3** Compare flats, rooms and neighbourhoods.
- **O4** Say where things are and where to put them (position and direction).
- **O5** Talk about neighbours and understand simple house notices.

**Target tasks:** read an ad · phone for a viewing · describe your own flat · tell movers where to put things · write to a landlord · read a house notice.

**Vocabulary:** **37 items** (33 productive, 4 receptive): searching and contract · rooms and equipment · properties · neighbourhood and house rules · positioning verbs.

**Grammar**
- **G1** `a2-adjektivendungen-indefinit` — endings after ein-/kein-/mein- (Nominativ, Akkusativ). Prerequisites: A1 `articles`, `nominativ`, `akkusativ`, `possessive`.
- **G2** `a2-komparativ-superlativ` — comparison. Prerequisite: G1.
- **G3** `a2-positionsverben` — stehen/stellen, liegen/legen, hängen. Prerequisite: A1 `wechsel`.

**Listening**
- **L1** Phone call tenant–landlord (formal, Sie): arrange a viewing, ask rent, extra costs, deposit. Questions: price, date, conditions.
- **L2** Two friends moving furniture (informal, du): where each item goes (Wo? / Wohin?). Questions: placement of three items, one correction.

**Reading**
- **R1** Housing ad (classified style, about 70 words) with typical abbreviations decoded in glosses. Tests: facts, suitability for a described person.
- **R2** Neighbour's note / house notice (about 70 words) using nominal rules (*Ruhe ab 22 Uhr*). Tests: what is required or allowed.

**Speaking**
- **S1** Viewing a flat with the landlord (Sie): ask about rooms, price, extras; compare with the present flat; say where your furniture will go.

**Writing**
- **W1** Formal e-mail answering an ad (about 60+ words): greeting, interest, two questions (incl. extra costs), one comparison, a viewing time, closing. First formal writing task after unit 11.

**RU / KY considerations**
- Russian has adjective agreement (endings by gender/case) — a useful bridge for G1; Kyrgyz adjectives do not decline, so the explanation must motivate endings through the article, with German examples first.
- Housing practice differs (deposit, extra costs, ownership vs. renting): *Kaution, Nebenkosten, Mietvertrag* need glosses that explain the German concept, chosen by native reviewers.
- Comparison: Russian -ее / Kyrgyz -раак patterns differ from German -er / am -sten; avoid false cognates in feedback.

**Dependencies:** A1 unit 5 vocabulary and `wechsel`; unit 12 pronouns; unit 11 Perfekt. Used later by 15 (adjective endings), 16, 18.

## Unit 14 — Gesundheit und Wohlbefinden

`a2-gesundheit` · unit id 14 · **Purpose:** talk about the body, illness and healthy living, understand and give advice, and cope with a doctor's visit.

**Objectives**
- **O1** Say what hurts and how you feel; describe symptoms and how long they have lasted.
- **O2** Make an appointment and answer a doctor's questions.
- **O3** Understand and give advice and instructions (*Nehmen Sie …*, *Trink …*).
- **O4** Say what you should, may or must not do.
- **O5** Explain what you do when something happens (*Wenn ich Fieber habe, …*).

**Target tasks:** phone a practice · describe symptoms to a doctor · follow dosage instructions · advise a friend · write a sick note e-mail · call an emergency number.

**Vocabulary:** **38 items** (34 productive, 4 receptive): body · complaints · doctor and medicine · healthy living.

**Grammar**
- **G1** `a2-imperativ` — du / ihr / Sie forms, irregular, separable, reflexive. Prerequisites: A1 `verb-position`, `separable`; `a2-reflexive-verben`.
- **G2** `a2-sollen-duerfen` — sollen, dürfen; müssen / können recycled. Prerequisite: A1 `modal`.
- **G3** `a2-wenn-saetze` — conditional / habitual `wenn`. Prerequisites: A1 `conjunctions`; `a2-ereignisse-verbinden`.

**Listening**
- **L1** Phone call to a practice (formal, Sie): make an appointment, state symptoms, receptionist's instructions. Questions: day/time, symptom, what to bring.
- **L2** A friend gives advice (informal, du) about a cold: what to do, what not to. Questions: three pieces of advice, one reason.

**Reading**
- **R1** Short package leaflet (about 60 words): dosage and warnings. Tests: scanning for amounts and prohibitions.
- **R2** Tips article "against stress" (about 80 words) with imperatives. Tests: main advice, detail.

**Speaking**
- **S1** At the doctor's (Sie): describe symptoms and duration, answer three questions, repeat the advice; patient role with a prompt card.

**Writing**
- **W1** Formal e-mail to a supervisor (about 50+ words): report illness, since when, how long absent, one `wenn` sentence (e.g. contact possibility), closing. Uses *mir tut … weh* with a Dativ pronoun.

**Revision exercises**

| E6 | order | G3 | wenn clause first, main clause with inversion |
| E7 | fill | G3 | verb in final position in a wenn clause |

**Final test**

**RU / KY considerations**
- Body-part and illness terms exist in both languages; set phrases differ (*Mir tut der Kopf weh* is a Dativ construction, unlike Russian «У меня болит голова» and Kyrgyz «Башым ооруйт») — explain as a pattern to be learned, with German first.
- Medical terms (Grippe, Salbe, Krankschreibung) need accurate glosses chosen by native reviewers; the app gives **language practice, not medical advice** (no dosage claims beyond what a leaflet text says).
- Imperative politeness: Russian *вы*/Kyrgyz *сиз* correspond to Sie, *сен* to du — a consistent mapping must be fixed by reviewers.

**Dependencies:** A1 unit 6/7/8 (Arzt, Termin, Apotheke); unit 12 (reflexive); unit 11 (`a2-praeteritum-sein-haben-modal`, Präteritum modals). Used later by 15, 17, 19, 20.

## Unit 15 — Essen, Einkaufen und Dienstleistungen

`a2-essen-einkaufen` · unit id 15 · **Purpose:** manage food, clothes and everyday services in shops, restaurants and workshops, politely and with the right quantities.

**Objectives**
- **O1** Buy food in the right quantities and ask what is available.
- **O2** Order in a restaurant, ask about ingredients, express taste.
- **O3** Buy clothes: size, fit, price, exchange.
- **O4** Use a service (hairdresser, repair, laundry) and make polite requests.
- **O5** Read menus, price lists, offers and receipts.

**Target tasks:** shop at a market · order a meal with a dietary need · try on and exchange a jacket · book a hairdresser or repair · complain politely · read a leaflet of offers.

**Vocabulary:** **38 items** (36 productive, 2 receptive): food · quantities and packaging · restaurant and taste · clothing · shopping and services.

**Grammar**
- **G1** `a2-adjektivendungen-definit` — endings after der/die/das/dieser/welcher and in the plural. Prerequisite: `a2-adjektivendungen-indefinit`.
- **G2** `a2-mengen-indefinitpronomen` — quantity expressions, *einen / keinen / welche*, *wie viel / wie viele*. Prerequisites: A1 `plural`, `akkusativ`, `negation`.
- **G3** `a2-hoefliche-bitten` — *Können Sie …? / Darf ich …? / Ich möchte … / Ich hätte gern …*. Prerequisites: `a2-personalpronomen-akk-dat`, `a2-sollen-duerfen`.

**Listening**
- **L1** Restaurant (formal, Sie): ordering, asking about ingredients and a vegetarian option, paying. Questions: order, restriction, total.
- **L2** Clothes shop (formal, Sie): sizes, trying on, price and discount. Questions: size, problem, decision.

**Reading**
- **R1** Menu extract with prices (non-continuous, about 60 words). Tests: choose by condition and budget.
- **R2** Shop flyer of offers plus a short service notice (about 70 words). Tests: prices, conditions, opening of services.

**Speaking**
- **S1** Phone a hairdresser or repair shop (Sie): make an appointment, state the wish, ask the price, confirm; polite chunks required.

**Writing**
- **W1** Formal e-mail to a shop (about 60+ words): bought item, problem (too small), request for exchange, polite closing; uses definite-article adjective endings and a quantity or price detail.

- Food, measure and clothing items often have local equivalents with different pack sizes and customs (bread, tea, meat); glosses must name the German item without importing local assumptions. *vegetarisch* and dietary concepts need native-checked wording.

## Unit 16 — Freizeit, Medien und Kultur

`a2-freizeit-medien` · unit id 16 · **Purpose:** talk about leisure, media habits and cultural events — preferences, opinions, plans with dates.

**Objectives**
- **O1** Say what you like to do and prefer, and compare.
- **O2** Talk about media use (phone, internet, TV, news).
- **O3** Give a simple opinion on a film, event or programme and recommend it.
- **O4** Understand and give event information: date, time, price.
- **O5** Say what you are interested in, look forward to or wait for.

**Target tasks:** discuss weekend plans · call a cinema or ticket office · read an events poster · write a short review or comment · talk about your phone and internet habits.

**Vocabulary:** **36 items** (33 productive, 3 receptive): hobbies and sport · media · culture and events · opinion and preference.

### 5. Distinct learning purpose
Each unit answers one question: 11 *who am I and what have I done*, 12 *who are the people around me*, 13 *where do I live*, 14 *how do I feel and what should I do*, 15 *how do I get what I need*, 16 *what do I like and do for fun*, 17 *what do I learn and work at, and why*, 18 *where do I go and what will I do*, 19 *how do I deal with the town and its offices*, 20 *how do we decide together*. No two share a primary function; overlaps are limited to recycled structures.

### 6. Assessment coverage and cumulative progression
* Per unit: 8 + 15 items; 10 grammar / 3 vocabulary / 2 reading; concept coverage in the final test is T1–T8 on the unit's own concepts (3 concepts: 3/3/2, 2 concepts: 4/4) plus T9–T10 recycling.
* Recycling in T9–T10 covers every earlier unit at least once across 12–20: 11 (Perfekt, Präteritum, `weil`), 12 (pronouns, reflexives), 13 (Komparativ), 14 (Imperativ, `wenn`), 16 (ordinals), 17 (`deshalb`). Units 15 and 18 are recycled indirectly through vocabulary and the level test (to be designed in a later stage).
* The **level test `level-a2`** and the **A2 placement items** (not part of this stage) must draw on all units; their specification should be based on this matrix.

### 7. Dependencies that should be taught earlier
See the grammar progression §7: Imperativ (I-1), dürfen/*man* (I-2). Both are written as decisions D-1 and D-5 in the Stage 7B report; the plan as written avoids them by using nominal notices and chunks in units 12–13.
